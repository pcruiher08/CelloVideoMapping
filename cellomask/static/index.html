Front face (3D only): no light on the neck, scroll, a hand or the bow in front of the top, and the edge pulled in so none reaches the ribs or the wall. Learned shape: the light uses the cello's own outline, fitted to the visible edges, so hands, knees or the bow cannot change it. Hands: painting over keeps the cello's whole shape lit while a hand, an arm or the bow covers part of it; leaving them dark cuts what is in front out of the light. Predict ahead: the light reaches the cello ~350 ms after the phone sees it; the mapping is moved to where the cello will be by then (0 = off; less overshoots less when the player stops suddenly).

Needs a depth source: the Kinect v2, or the iPhone LiDAR (CelloDepth app → Connect), mounted rigidly beside the projector. Solves the projector's lens and position in 3D from the stripes plus depth; the lit area must include different depths (cello in front of the wall, some floor). Also refits the flat mapping above.

Lights the cello white and, all around its outline, pulls the edge in where light spills past it and out where it stops short. Kept with the 3D scan.

Lights the cello white, sees where the light lands, and shifts it onto the cello (Shift X/Y). Fixes what the calibration leaves at the cello's spot, or the camera being knocked slightly.
